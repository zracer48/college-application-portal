What Is the Common Application, and How Does It Work?

The Common Application (Common App) is an online application platform used by many colleges and universities. Students complete a shared application and then respond to each college’s additional questions and requirements. The Common App typically includes: ●​ Personal and family information, including household and parent/guardian education details. ●​

Education history, high schools attended, current coursework, grades, and academic distinctions. ●​ Testing information, when students choose or are required to report scores. ●​ Activities, responsibilities, work experience, and honors. ●​

A personal essay and college-specific questions or supplemental writing, when required. How the process works:

Create or access a Common App account using an email address you regularly check.
Complete the shared profile, family, education, testing, activities, and writing sections.
Add colleges and review each college’s questions, deadlines, and submission requirements.
Request or coordinate recommendations and school documents when applicable.
Review each application carefully, submit it, and confirm submission in the applicant portal.

Important: Adding a college to Common App does not submit an application. Each college must be reviewed and submitted individually.

What Is the University of California Application?

The University of California (UC) undergraduate application is the application used to apply to UC campuses. It is separate from the Common Application. The UC application typically includes: ●​ Personal information, residency-related questions, and family information. ●​

High schools attended and academic history, including courses and grades. ●​ College coursework taken during high school, if applicable. ●​ Activities, awards, work experience, and educational preparation programs. ●​

Personal Insight Questions (PIQs) and campus/major selections. How the process works:

Create or access a UC application account.
Choose the appropriate application term and select UC campuses and intended majors.
Complete the personal, academic, activities, and PIQ sections.
Review all responses and submit the UC application by the applicable deadline.
Monitor email and campus portals for any required follow-up.

Important: The UC application is not a Common App supplement. Students applying to both systems must complete and submit each separately.

Please provide the following information before your UC application planning and final review sessions.

Personal and educational information

●​ Legal name, contact information, birth date, and applicable residency details. ●​ Every high school attended, with attendance dates and school details. ●​ A complete high school transcript, including grades 9–11 and available grade 12 coursework.

Detailed academic records

●​ Course names, grade levels, term grades, and course lengths as shown on academic records. ●​ Current and planned senior-year courses. ●​ Records for summer school, online courses, transfers, repeated courses, and courses taken outside the main high school.

●​ College or Dual Enrollment transcripts and course details, if applicable.

Activities and written responses

●​ Activities, honors, volunteering, employment, and educational preparation experiences. ●​ Dates, participation time, responsibilities, achievements, and any required award details. ●​ Ideas, outlines, or drafts for the UC Personal Insight Questions.

Campus and major planning

●​ UC campus list and intended majors, including alternate majors where a campus permits them. ●​ Any special program or supplemental application requirements associated with a major. Please alert our team if your school uses an unusual grading system, you studied outside the United States, or you have courses that do not appear on your main transcript.:

How Do You Review UC Academic History for Accuracy?

The UC application requires careful course-by-course reporting. Students must compare every entry against their transcripts and other academic records. What to check: ●​ All schools attended and their attendance dates are correct. ●​

Courses and grades are entered under the correct academic year and term. ●​ Course names and course categories are reported according to the UC application instructions. ●​ Current and planned grade 12 courses are listed appropriately. ●​

Applicable college courses, summer courses, and outside coursework are included. ●​ Repeated courses, nontraditional grades, and unusual academic situations follow UC instructions. Understanding A–G coursework UC uses subject-area requirements commonly called A–G. Students should check how their completed and planned coursework satisfies the relevant subject areas and campus eligibility requirements.

Common mistakes to avoid: ●​ Do not calculate or enter grades differently from the application instructions. ●​ Do not omit coursework simply because it is repeated, difficult, or taken outside the main school. ●​

Do not assume a course is UC-approved without checking applicable course guidance. Important: UC application rules for reporting coursework can differ from Common App and STARS. Follow the UC application’s instructions for the relevant academic year and school. A–G subject areas: A = history/social science; B = English; C = mathematics; D = laboratory science; E = language other than English; F = visual and performing arts; G = college-preparatory elective. Course eligibility and reporting rules vary by school and applicant background.

How Do Activities, Awards, and PIQs Work?

The UC application asks students to describe their experiences and reflect on their contributions, interests, and growth. These sections are not identical to the Common App activities list or personal essay. Activities and awards ●​ Organize academic preparation, extracurricular activities, volunteer work, employment, and awards in the categories provided.

●​ Use specific descriptions of what you did, your level of involvement, and the impact or outcome. ●​ Report participation dates and time commitments accurately. ●​ Include meaningful family responsibilities or work experiences when applicable.

Personal Insight Questions (PIQs) First-year applicants generally answer four of eight PIQ prompts, with a 350-word maximum per response. Confirm the prompts and limits in the current application. ●​ Choose distinct topics that collectively show different aspects of the student. ●​

Use concrete examples and reflection rather than repeating an activities description. ●​ Answer the actual question and stay within the word limit. ●​ Ensure the final responses sound like the student and reflect their real experiences. Important: A Common App personal essay should not automatically be pasted into a UC PIQ. The formats and purposes

differ. UC activities and awards: The application permits up to 20 entries across its activities and awards categories. Quality matters more than filling every slot; include responsibilities, service, employment, academic preparation, and meaningful accomplishments. UC Personal Insight Questions: First-year applicants answer four of eight prompts, with no more than 350 words per response. Choose distinct, authentic examples and review all four final responses before submission.

Testing: UC does not consider SAT or ACT scores in first-year admission decisions or for awarding scholarships. Follow the application’s instructions for any other reportable exams or scores.

4. STARS: Self-Reporting Grades

View / Print PDF

Some institutions use STARS (Self-reported Transcript and Academic Record System) or a similar process to collect coursework and grades directly from students.

How does it work?

You may need to enter courses, grades, credits, grading scales, and school history exactly as shown on your transcript. A college may require the record to be linked to its application or portal.

What we need and what we do

Your team may ask for a complete, readable high-school transcript and clarification about unusual courses, repeated classes, dual enrollment, or grading conventions. We can help organize and review the entries, but you should verify that the record matches your official transcript before certifying it.

How to review and correct errors

Compare each academic year, course title, grade, and credit with the transcript. If you notice an error, contact the A+ Skills team promptly with the school, course, academic year, and correct information. Whether a submitted record can be edited depends on the system and college.

Midyear and final updates

Some schools require updates after first-semester grades or a final transcript after graduation. Do not assume the initial self-reported record satisfies every later requirement. Watch the college portal for additional instructions.

5. Other Self-Reported Academic Record Systems

View / Print PDF
UCF SPARK

UCF uses the SPARK academic record process for applicable applicants. Follow the instructions and timing in your UCF applicant portal; it may become available after the main application is submitted. Enter courses and grades exactly as required.

University of California academic history

UC applications include detailed academic history and course reporting. Check course categories, terms, and grades carefully, particularly for out-of-state, honors, AP, IB, or dual-enrollment courses. The UC process is distinct from STARS.

Common App courses and grades

Some colleges require the Common App Courses & Grades section; others do not. If required, report your academic record using the transcript and the instructions for that college. Do not assume completing one school's system automatically satisfies another's.

6. Florida Residency Documentation (Florida Residents Only)

View / Print PDF

Florida applicants seeking in-state tuition classification may need to provide information about the person establishing Florida residency, often a parent or legal guardian for dependent students.

Information your team may request
  • The date Florida domicile was established (month/day/year), when known.
  • Evidence such as a Florida driver license, vehicle registration, or voter registration, depending on the school's requirements.
  • Additional identifying details or supporting records requested by the college's official residency process.

Documentation requirements and acceptable evidence vary. A Florida address or driver's license alone does not guarantee in-state tuition classification.

When is residency documentation submitted?

Timing differs by school. Some institutions request residency information during the application, while others ask for it after submission through an applicant portal. Follow each college's checklist; your A+ Skills team can help identify what is outstanding.

When should you contact us?

Contact the team if you are unsure which domicile date to use, the residency claimant is not a parent, your family's circumstances have changed, or you are not a U.S. citizen but have lawful Florida residency or another potentially qualifying status. These situations may require institution-specific guidance.

Privacy: Use the institution's secure process for identity documents. Do not send a driver's license or other sensitive records by ordinary email unless your team specifically confirms a suitable secure method.

8. Honors Programs and Colleges

View / Print PDF
What is an honors program?

Honors programs may offer smaller classes, advising, research opportunities, special seminars, priority registration, or residential communities. Benefits and commitments vary substantially by college.

Is honors right for you?

Consider whether you enjoy academically challenging coursework, want access to honors-specific opportunities, and can meet the program's ongoing requirements. Being interested in a college does not mean you must apply to its honors program. Honors admission is often a separate decision from general university admission.

Applications, essays, and deadlines

Some colleges ask for honors materials with the main application; others invite students to apply afterward. Deadlines can be earlier than regular admissions deadlines. Set aside dedicated time for any additional essay or interview preparation and confirm requirements for each institution.

10. Post-Submission: Application Portals, Emails, and Application Monitoring

Set up every applicant portal

After submission, watch for a message from each college explaining how to activate its applicant portal. Keep the college name, official portal URL, application ID, and account email organized in your assigned application worksheet. Store passwords in a secure password manager—not in a publicly shared spreadsheet.

Organize college emails

Create an email label or folder for each college and check spam or junk folders regularly. Save portal invitations, verification codes, requests for materials, financial-aid notices, and admissions decisions.

Why items may initially show as missing

It is common for portals to show incomplete checklists shortly after an application is submitted. Transcripts, recommendations, and scores may take several business days or longer to be matched. Processing varies by school and delivery method; a short delay is not necessarily a problem.

How to report portal updates to A+ Skills

Keep your application worksheet updated with each portal's status, the last date checked, outstanding items, and important messages. Let your team know when a deadline is near, an item remains missing beyond the school's expected processing time, or the portal requests action. What to do when a college contacts you

Read the full message and verify the sender or official portal. Forward or summarize time-sensitive requests to your team, especially anything involving deadlines, residency, transcripts, testing, or scholarships. Do not ignore messages just because the main application was already submitted.

11. Sending Official Standardized Test Scores: SAT, ACT, and CLT Exams

View / Print PDF
Which scores should be sent?

Each college has its own testing policy. Some permit self-reporting; others require official reports from the testing organization. Your A+ Skills team can review your scores and the colleges on your list, discuss which reports make sense, and confirm your choice before proceeding.

How official reports are ordered

SAT reports are typically ordered through College Board and ACT reports through ACT. Where accepted, CLT reporting follows the receiving institution's requirements. Your team can guide the process or help place orders with your authorization. Any fees and payment or reimbursement arrangements should be confirmed in advance.

Standard versus rush delivery

Rush options, when available, do not guarantee that a college will immediately process a report. We should evaluate the school's deadline, accepted reporting methods, and normal processing time before paying extra. Check the official testing provider and college for current options.

12. College Housing Applications and Priority Registration

View / Print PDF
Why housing deserves early attention

At some colleges, housing availability or preferred residence halls can be competitive. Submitting an eligible housing application early may preserve options, but it does not guarantee placement or admission.

What to review before applying
  • When the housing application opens and whether admission or enrollment deposits are required.
  • Application fees, deposits, cancellation terms, and refund deadlines.
  • Room preferences, roommate selection, meal plans, and special accommodation processes.
  • Whether applying for housing creates any binding financial obligation.
UF and other competitive housing situations

Pay particular attention to institution-specific housing timelines, especially at universities where desirable housing options fill quickly. Your team can help you identify the correct official housing portal and review the steps before you commit.
